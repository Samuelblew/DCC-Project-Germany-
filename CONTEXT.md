# DCC Domain Context

## Mission Generator

- **Mission Plan intake**: The module that turns mission chat into a validated mission plan. It owns AI object generation, text repair, fallback mission plan creation, and explicit user intent enforcement before a campaign is built.
- **GCI**: Native MiG-29A KRU intercept link. Mission generation tiles `GCI_station_MiG29` vans on each coalition's airfields (WAVE 1–10 friendly, 11–20 enemy), runs Activate GCI, and links compatible IADS search radars inside 250 km. Playing red puts NATO GCI on the opponent; playing blue puts Warsaw Pact GCI on the opponent. Culling keeps only stations on the SAM corridor in the .miz; the campaign map still shows both networks. Other aircraft keep optional F10 BRAA picture calls.
- **DCS Bubble**: `campaignParams.worldMode` chooses the placement. `corridor` (default) keeps ground and SAM along the route in the `.miz`. `bubble` leaves ground, SAM and AI flights out of the `.miz` and the mission script spawns them beside the player from `dcc_bubble.lua` (rewritten during the sortie), each kind at its own radius, then destroys them past 120% of that radius without counting a kill. The player's own flight stays in the mission. The gear beside the cheat skull sets the mode for the next mission. Full notes in `docs/dcs-bubble-context.md`.

## Campaign Simulation

- **Campaign Tick**: The module that advances an active campaign by applying fast and logic phases in the correct order for both coalitions.
- **Ground Deployment**: The Module that exclusively assigns campaign formations to curated tactical sites and exact unit slots. Objective ownership remains the land-campaign authority; this Module owns legal placement, reservations, stable unit continuity, withdrawal routing, and local recovery transfer.
- **Combat Viability**: The shared Interface that projects formation count, combat power, CAS eligibility, retreat thresholds, and reformation eligibility. ATO planning, manual mission intake, active package validation, and the ground campaign must use this Interface instead of local survivor-count rules.
- **Troop Extraction**: A helicopter operation for exact surviving members of an isolated withdrawing infantry formation. Pickup removes those stable assets from the battlefield; delivery dissolves them into the destination's local Replacement Pool.
- **CASEVAC**: A theatre-independent helicopter operation for wounded personnel collected at a casualty point. It is separate from CSAR and Troop Extraction and never recovers destroyed equipment.
- **Replacement Pool**: Personnel and equipment stored at one recovery node. Pools are local to the owned supply graph so reconstitution cannot teleport assets between disconnected objectives.
- **Naval Campaign**: The Module that owns persistent surface-group position, exact ship continuity, damage, fuel, ammunition, cargo, operating state, and Falklands landing prerequisites.
- **IADS Network**: The Module that owns persistent early-warning radar identity, position, and destroyed state. Mission generation consumes this state and must never recreate destroyed radar sites.
- **Escalation**: The Module that owns the campaign's technical generation (Korea → Vietnam → Late CW → Modern) on German soil, how long each generation lasts in campaign days, the USAFE/GSFG aircraft lists, and which IADS equipment those Sentry positions field. The player picks it from the era menu; faction pick is skipped because the ladder already locks USAFE/GSFG presets. The scenario calendar date does not jump years. Ground tempo, ATO caps, and commander posture follow the generation. A stage change re-equips both sides, sends previous-generation AI home, and updates coalition faction ids; the last generation stays until the scenario ends.
- **Objective Graph**: Theatre land connections that constrain ground marches to the next adjacent objective. Mission generation follows the same edges so DCS ground routes stay on the authored graph. Geography is era-independent; Late CW and Escalation share the Fulda hop net.
- **Commander Posture**: The live `operationProfile` derived from who holds that graph and the current generation. On the Fulda graph a stalemate is still two-sided (initiative side breakthrough, the other defense-crisis). A large Late CW/Modern lead becomes air-superiority for the leader. Korea/Vietnam stay cautious and never take automatic fighter sweep. Must not raise extra DCS units.
- **ATO Package**: One strike Time-on-Target with escort flights offset from it (SEAD TOT−2 min, DEAD TOT−1, CAP TOT−8). Korea-era lists without SEAD/DEAD still get strike+CAP. Standalone DEAD is only for emitters not already on a strike package.
- **BARCAP**: Automatic CAP patrols a contact edge of the land graph (friendly side of the FLOT), not the home airfield. Depth follows commander posture and era (Korea stays shorter of the line). Theatres without a land graph keep the airbase-lane CAP. A defending side prefers that BARCAP/CAS over deep strike.
- **Interdiction**: Destroyed depots, ammo, fuel, C2, and power at an objective raise `supply.logistics.interdiction`. That throttles inbound supply and resource gain, and at ≥0.6 the barracks there stop raising new columns. An interdicted garrison holds instead of launching a new assault. Columns already on the road from that origin abort unless they have reached battle radius. Adjacent hops prefer an unchoked neighbor but still assault a starved town if it is the only hop. Owned retreat routes skip smashed towns as transit. Attrition ATO prefers those logistics structures. No extra DCS units.
- **BAI**: Automatic CAS prefers enemy formations on the land-graph FLOT. Rear idle garrisons are left alone while a contact edge exists. Theatres without a graph keep armed recon.
- **OCA**: ATO runway-attack packages crater an enemy airfield in campaign state (`recoveryUntil`). That field cannot launch until it recovers. Korea F-86 and Late CW F-4 both fly it; escorts follow the same ToT rules as strike. No extra DCS units.
- **AWACS**: ATO keeps one orbit behind the FLOT when the faction list has AWACS (Late CW E-3A / A-50, Modern). Korea and Vietnam lists are empty, so they stay on GCI and the mission generator does not invent an E-3. Refueling lists are empty across factions — do not invent tankers. If a campaign AWACS is already in the .miz, the generator does not inject a second immortal one.
- **Intercept**: ATO scrambles against an inbound enemy strike/OCA/CAS/DEAD package aimed at a friendly objective. One scramble per side. If BARCAP already sits on that airspace, no extra scramble. Empty `Intercept` lists fall back to CAP aircraft (F-86 Korea, F-14 Late CW). No extra DCS units.
- **Fighter Sweep**: Automatic ATO pushes past BARCAP onto the hostile side of the FLOT when posture is breakthrough or air-superiority and tempo is not cautious. Korea/Vietnam stay on BARCAP. Empty `Fighter Sweep` lists fall back to CAP aircraft. Manual sweep still exists.
- **South Atlantic Mission Adapter**: The Adapter that emits and imports South Atlantic countries, persistent ships, carrier bases, exact ground slots, withdrawals, extraction zones, and CASEVAC results without folding naval state into generic ground state.
